Big Sludge — Splunk

Fast threat-hunting and evidence workflow for the tryout's Splunk Enterprise box.

DoneAlmost DoneReturn to Complete

Get Oriented

Log into Splunk and confirm the indexer is receiving events.
Identify the Windows and Ubuntu hosts/sourcetypes so you can filter quickly.
Record a normal baseline early so later activity has something to compare against.

Fast Searches

Search by host first: host=<hostname>.
Filter authentication activity, failed logins, sudo/SSH events, and Windows logon events.
Look for unusual process creation, new services, scheduled tasks, account changes, and privilege changes.
Look for spikes in errors or events immediately before/after a scored service fails.

Windows Threat Hunting

Review failed/successful logons and unexpected source addresses.
Look for new users or changes to privileged groups.
Look for service creation, scheduled tasks, PowerShell activity, and Defender events.

Linux Threat Hunting

Review SSH logins, failed authentication, sudo usage, and account changes.
Correlate suspicious login times with auth.log/journal activity.
Look for persistence clues such as cron/systemd activity and unexpected service starts.

Incident Evidence

When you find something suspicious, record timestamp, host, user, source IP, process/service, and relevant event details.
Take screenshots or copy key event details before remediation if useful for the incident report.
After remediation, verify similar activity stops and note what mitigation you applied.

Do Not Break

Do not disable the Black Team-controlled Splunk forwarding from Windows or Ubuntu.
Your own Splunk indexer has no scored service, so use it aggressively for visibility—but avoid unnecessary changes that cost you time.
